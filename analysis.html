Methodology

Analytical Approach

1

Discrete Random Variable Classification

Annual rainfall for each subdivision-year pair is classified into three discrete categories, Excess, Normal, and Deficient, based on whether it exceeds the 75th percentile, falls between the 25th and 75th percentile, or lies below the 25th percentile. This defines a discrete probability mass function over the state space.

P(X = k) = n_k / N, k ∈ {Excess, Normal, Deficient}
2

Continuous Distribution Fitting

Monsoon (Jun-Sep) rainfall across all records is modelled as a Normal distribution fitted by maximum likelihood estimation (μ̂ = sample mean, σ̂² = mean squared deviation, dividing by n). The histogram below shows the chosen subdivision's monsoon rainfall with its own Normal and lognormal MLE fits, so the two models can be compared.

Statistical Independence Test

January and July rainfall are binarised above and below the median. Joint probabilities are compared with the product of marginals under the independence assumption. The probability gap and the Pearson correlation (with its p-value) are computed live below. Zero correlation would only rule out linear dependence.

5

Polynomial Trend Fitting

Annual rainfall time-series for any subdivision is fitted with degree-1, -2, or -3 polynomials via least-squares, computed live in-browser using Gaussian elimination on the normal equations. For Kerala the linear slope is -2.84 mm/year (p = 0.016) but R² is only about 0.05, so the trend explains roughly 5% of the year-to-year variance. Adjusted R² and a slope t-test are shown next to the chart.
