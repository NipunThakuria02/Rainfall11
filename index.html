Meteorological Enquiry · IMD 1901-2015

Why probability theory
+ Indian rainfall?

India's agricultural output, feeding over a billion people, is driven almost entirely by the Southwest Monsoon. Yet annual rainfall is deeply stochastic: it varies by an order of magnitude between arid zones and the eastern hills, and fluctuates sharply year to year.

Classical statistics gives us tools, Bayes' theorem, normal distribution fits, correlation tests, and polynomial trend models, to move beyond intuition and quantify this uncertainty with mathematical precision.

This study applies those tools to 115 years of IMD sub-divisional rainfall records to answer: How predictable is India's rain? Are winter and monsoon rainfall independent? Is there a long-term trend?

Research Question 1
Is monsoon rainfall a reliable predictor of annual totals?

Bayes' theorem gives P(high annual | high monsoon) = 0.910, versus a 25% baseline. Jun-Sep supplies about 75% of pooled annual rainfall.

Research Question 2
Are January and July rainfall statistically independent?

Pearson r = -0.052 (p ≈ 0.0009) and probability gap = 0.018: statistically detectable but practically negligible dependence, consistent with winter and monsoon rain having different drivers.

Research Question 3
Is there a measurable long-term trend in annual rainfall?

Polynomial least-squares fit for Kerala gives slope = -2.84 mm/yr (p = 0.016), but R² is only about 0.05: a significant but weak decline masked by year-to-year variability.
